## AI use is a stated requirement

Postings in healthcare, e-commerce, and finance expect AI to already be part of how the candidate works:

- HelloFresh: "Active usage of AI as a core part of your daily workflow"
- Evinova: "Content designer with AI fluency, not necessarily a technical expert"
- Wealthsimple: "From shipping your own code changes to prototyping and research"

It sits in the requirements, next to craft and portfolio.

## Judgment is what the person still brings

When postings say what a person adds on top of the tool, they name judgment and taste. Gap puts it most plainly.

> AI will write plausible copy at volume all day, which makes judgment about what is worth saying the scarce skill rather than the throughput.

Others say it in their own terms:

- CoLab: "act as Editor in Chief: orchestrating AI agents and applying editorial judgment across outputs"
- Soda: "Taste is the bar. Anti-slop is the whole point."

## Some postings design where that judgment happens

A narrower set writes the human review step into the job itself:

- Bank of America: "Act as a human-in-the-loop for emerging platforms by defining, reviewing, and improving prompts, responses, agent behaviors"
- Huge: "frameworks for governance, training data readiness, and human-in-the-loop review"
- Intuit: "AI answer evaluations at scale with LLM-as-a-judge models and human-in-the-loop"

<aside>

AI fluency used to be the thing that got you noticed. Now it's the floor. Postings list it next to Figma, and nobody gets hired for knowing Figma.

What's left is the part that was always the job — deciding what's good. Gap's line keeps pulling me back. When a tool can write copy all day, knowing what's worth saying is the skill.

The postings that go furthest also decide where judgment sits in the workflow. That's content design applied to its own process, and it's where I'd point anyone trying to grow into systems work.

The real work now lives in the moments you overrule the model, and in knowing why.

</aside>
