What content design jobs are telling us

A structured dataset of 85 senior content design, UX writing, and content systems roles — and the signals they send.

Explore the data View on GitHub (opens in a new tab)

Content design is evolving. Teams are shrinking. Roles and responsibilities are expanding. The skills needed to succeed in the future are starting to appear.

Explore the data — skill signals and responsibility clusters drawn from senior content design job postings

Emerging patterns and themes

AI fluency expected
68%

AI fluency expected

Appears in 58 of 85 roles

AI fluency is listed as a baseline in most roles we're tracking. If you're not actively working with AI tools, this is the most urgent gap the data shows.

Governance emphasized
39%

Governance emphasized

Appears in 33 of 85 roles

Governance is the primary deliverable in these roles, not a background process. Companies are hiring for the ability to set standards and make them stick across teams.

Enabler, not bottleneck
35%

Enabler, not bottleneck

Appears in 30 of 85 roles

These roles are hiring someone to build systems that let others write well, not someone to write everything themselves. A consistent signal that the solo-writer model is losing ground.

Model behavior design
32%

Model behavior design

Appears in 27 of 85 roles

A growing number of roles are asking content designers to shape how AI models actually respond, not just write around them. An emerging space where content design and machine learning overlap directly.

Central standards function
31%

Central standards function

Appears in 26 of 85 roles

Content standards owned by one central or horizontal function, with distributed teams consuming them inside set guardrails. Roles describe sitting above channels, acting as a single accountable owner, or publishing playbooks other teams adapt. It's a structural answer to where content work sits, distinct from embedding writers directly in product teams.

Content systems for marketers
14%

Content systems for marketers

Appears in 12 of 85 roles

Roles that specialize in content architecture, taxonomy governance, and AI output standards are being hired into marketing orgs, not just product and design. The systems responsibilities are substantive. What's different is where they sit.

Content systems design

92%

Content systems design

Appears in 78 of 85 roles

The most common cluster in the dataset. Companies want someone who designs content as a system: frameworks, patterns, reusable templates that scale past a single screen.

Enablement & team upskilling

80%

Enablement & team upskilling

Appears in 68 of 85 roles

Content design teams are shrinking, and it's increasingly common to be a one-person centralized resource. The roles we're tracking are hiring for the ability to build playbooks, guidelines, and tools that scale content quality across teams, not to write everything yourself.

AI tooling & automation

74%

AI tooling & automation

Appears in 63 of 85 roles

Building and operationalizing AI-powered workflows is now a stated expectation in most roles we're tracking. This goes beyond using AI tools. Companies want someone who can evaluate, configure, and own the workflow end to end.

AI-generated content governance

46%

AI-generated content governance

Appears in 39 of 85 roles

As AI-generated content scales, someone has to own the quality standards. Rubrics, prompt guidelines, tone constraints, guardrails. This responsibility is growing fast in the dataset.

Terminology & language governance

38%

Terminology & language governance

Appears in 32 of 85 roles

Companies are asking content designers to own the language layer, building glossaries, controlled vocabularies, and a single source of truth for product language. The pattern holds across industries, not one sector.

Taxonomy & information architecture

34%

Taxonomy & information architecture

Appears in 29 of 85 roles

Classification and information architecture are showing up in content design roles that didn't ask for them a few years ago. Content designers who can build and maintain structured content models are increasingly valuable, especially where AI needs clean inputs.
